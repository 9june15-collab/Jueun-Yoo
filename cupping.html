What I’ve been looking at? Myths about mackerel, and the images and artifacts indicating those things. What are the things that I can see through those findings? How myths made is really depends on what the observer observed and experienced about the things. For example, in Korea, there’ s geographical separation caused by the steep mountains that separates capital area and the east sea. Therefore, normal folks rarely see the school of ‘flashy’ mackerel swimming around the sea. Rather, most of them were salted and transported through the cities, similarly as the example from Japan. It is much easier to be rotten in the northeastern Asia area because of relatively humid weather. So, in my home area, mackerel has much meaning as foods that shows humbleness of working class. So I can talk more about ‘dead’ mackerels. I was a bit surprised that the findings that came from the other area has much stories about ‘alive’ mackerel. The slang of Maquereau can be assumed to be originated from the behavior of mackerel herd, as my interviewee refered. It could also explain the finding from Solomon Island, as acessaries that shows the strength of mackerel. This phenomenon would be directed from many causes like flat geography, less-humid weather, develoment of ports or something like that. So the question(s)? As a designer, I cannot investigate all the causes. I’m not an historian like Historical Vagabond. But I can see what happens if those contexts combined and mixed. Would it make another myths? Would it make people see mackerel in different view? Also, the methodology can also be questions. How should I remix these contexts of the dead and alive?
